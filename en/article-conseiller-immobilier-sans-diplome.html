Can you become a real estate advisor without a degree?

By Guillaume RoquePublished and checked on 21 September 2026

The answer in brief

Yes. In France, an individual can work as an independent real estate advisor under commercial agent status without a specific real estate degree. They must, however, act on behalf of a professional who holds the real estate professional card, under a mandate, with RSAC registration and a CCI collaborator certificate requested by the cardholder. This route does not allow them to present themselves as an estate agent holding their own card.

Independent advisor and estate agent: two different legal positions

In everyday language, the terms are often mixed up. Legally, the real estate commercial agent—also called an independent real estate negotiator—negotiates in the name of and on behalf of the principal. The professional cardholder oversees the mandate and requests the certificate authorising the individual collaborator to negotiate.

Official references consulted: Service Public Entreprendre, ‘Becoming an independent commercial agent’, verified on 6 February 2026 and CCI France, collaborator certificate.

The key distinction

Without a specific real estate degree, the route described here is for an individual working as a commercial agent for a professional cardholder. If the activity is carried on through a company, Service Public states that the legal entity must itself hold the professional card. The chosen legal structure should therefore be checked before completing any formalities.

What you still need to do without a degree

Starting without a dedicated degree does not mean starting without a legal framework. Before working, check at least the following steps with your chosen network and the relevant authorities:

  • sign a commercial agent contract or mandate setting out the working and remuneration terms;
  • choose a legal structure suited to your situation and complete the business formalities;
  • register with the Special Register of Commercial Agents (RSAC);
  • obtain the collaborator certificate requested from the CCI by the professional cardholder;
  • stay within the scope of your duties: a real estate commercial agent may not, in particular, receive client funds, provide legal advice or manage an estate agency.

The formalities and limits applying to an individual real estate commercial agent are detailed by Service Public Entreprendre. For more detail on the process: status and formalities for an independent real estate advisor.

Why the professional card changes the answer

Opening and managing your own agency under your own professional card is a different route. The CCI requires evidence of professional competence, as well as compliance with the other conditions attached to the card. Depending on the situation, competence may be based on a qualification or on professional experience provided for in the legislation. It is therefore essential to distinguish between starting as an independent advisor and obtaining your own estate agent professional card.

Official source: CCI France, application for a real estate professional card.

Without a degree, which skills should you develop first?

Your priority is not to compensate for the lack of a qualification title, but to build a reliable professional practice. A structured start can be organised around four areas:

  1. Professional framework: the principal’s role, limits of authorisation, professional ethics, data protection and the documents used.
  2. Commercial method: prospecting, understanding the client’s plans, following up contacts and factual reporting.
  3. Real estate knowledge: local market, valuation, finance, surveys and the transaction process, without going beyond your permitted role.
  4. Organisation: start-up budget, available time, training schedule and activity indicators.

For the professionals concerned, the continuing training decree provides for fourteen hours per year or forty-two hours over three consecutive years. Check how it applies to your situation and which evidence is required with the cardholder.

Official legislation: Decree no. 2016-173 of 18 February 2016 on continuing training for real estate professionals. iad also presents its training pathway and mentor support on its official training page.

Five checks before choosing a network

  • Ask who holds the professional card and who will submit your certificate application.
  • Read the contract, fees and remuneration terms before committing.
  • Ask for the initial training programme and how your progress will be monitored.
  • Check who will answer your questions during your first cases and how quickly.
  • Prepare a prudent personal budget: the activity is self-employed and commissions depend on completed transactions, never on memberships.

This checklist cannot predict your results. It simply helps you compare a framework, tools and support with your actual situation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which qualification is required to become an independent real estate advisor?

In France, the route for an individual real estate commercial agent does not require a specific real estate degree. It does require, among other things, a mandate, RSAC registration and a collaborator certificate requested by the professional cardholder.

Can you call yourself an estate agent without a professional card?

No. A real estate commercial agent acts on behalf of a professional cardholder. This role must not be confused with an estate agent who holds their own card.

Is training useful when no degree is required at the outset?

Yes. The role requires commercial, legal, technical and interpersonal skills. Structured initial training and regular support help develop these skills without promising any commercial result.

Are these rules the same outside France?

No. This article describes the French framework. Each country, and sometimes each state, applies its own operating requirements. Check the rules of the relevant subsidiary and territory before starting.
