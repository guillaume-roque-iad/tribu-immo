Switching real estate networks: how should you plan your first 30 days?

Guillaume Roque · Published 16 November 2026

Changing property networks involves more than adopting new tools. Experienced advisers can use the first thirty days to clarify commitments, learn procedures and work with their new team. This is a practical framework, not a guarantee of results.

Before the move: review commitments

Read your commercial agency agreement, notice provisions, commissions and ongoing cases. Seek written clarification of uncertain points. Client instructions and databases do not automatically follow the adviser: review each case under contracts and data protection requirements.

Week one: understand procedures

Identify the right contacts, check your authorisations before working and learn the new tools for instructions, compliance, marketing and case management. Ask for demonstrations instead of repeating previous habits. Do not copy client files from a former business.

Week two: set priorities

List useful training, authorised meetings and cases actually assigned to you. Set a date and a responsible contact for each task. Clear follow-up helps distinguish activity from progress.

Week three: make use of mentoring

Discuss differences between your previous practices and the new procedures. Ask for feedback on a real situation and select one improvement. iad describes online and in-person training with sponsor or mentor support: confirm which programme applies to you.

Week four: carry out a review

Check tool access, training completed, the quality of responses and commitments met. Record obstacles and next steps. Thirty days or a certain number of meetings cannot guarantee sales or income.

After the first month: adjust

Continue learning, confirm the costs and services in your actual contract and set realistic priorities. Compliance and cooperation also matter, not just commercial outcomes.

Frequently asked questions

Do previous client instructions transfer automatically?

No. Check the relevant agreements and permissions before contacting clients.

Does an experienced adviser still need training?

Yes, particularly on new tools, procedures and compliance requirements.

Is thirty days enough to become independent?

Not necessarily. It is an initial review point, not a guaranteed deadline.
