How can you keep sellers informed, even when there are no viewings?

By Guillaume RoquePublished and checked on 14 September 2026

Your method at a glance

Agree an update schedule with each seller, keep the facts in one record and end every report with a dated next step. Even without viewings, explain what you have done, what you have observed and what you suggest testing. This is an organisational method to adapt to your portfolio and the commitments in your agency agreements, not a guarantee of a sale.

1. Schedule an update, rather than just intending to send one

At the start of marketing, agree the preferred channel, the date of the next update and the topics to review. A weekly report can be a starting point if you can sustain that frequency. Block out time in your diary and record the next deadline in each property file. Feedback after a viewing complements this appointment; it does not replace it.

2. Keep a short, actionable record for each property

Your record should let you prepare a conversation without rereading every message. Keep these five fields in your usual tool:

  • Period and actions: reporting dates, checked listings, updated presentation and work actually carried out.
  • Observed results: enquiries, viewings and offers, with their source. Unavailable data stays ‘unavailable’, not zero.
  • Feedback: positive points, stated concerns and unanswered questions, without turning an impression into an intention to buy.
  • Analysis: hypotheses to check, separate from facts. Few enquiries alone do not show that price is the only problem.
  • Next step: proposed action, person responsible and review date.

3. After a viewing, report the facts before drawing conclusions

Ask what works, what is holding the person back and what they see as the next step. Note their answers, then prepare an accurate update for the seller. If the buyer has not replied yet, say so and schedule a follow-up. Avoid ‘very positive viewing’ without specifics: liking a room is not an offer.

4. Without viewings, review the marketing

First check that the listing is visible, that photos and information match the property and that enquiries have been handled. Then compare available feedback with the previous period on a like-for-like basis. Do not add views from different portals as though they were unique people. Suggest a specific adjustment and set a date to review its effects.

Fictional example of an update without viewings

There were no viewings during the period reviewed. The listings have been checked. Statistics from one portal are unavailable, so I cannot draw a conclusion about visibility there. I suggest clarifying the description of access to the property and reviewing progress on the agreed date. This scenario illustrates a method; it is not a case handled by Guillaume.

5. Use team discussions to move an action forward

Bring an anonymised case and a specific question: how should this concern be phrased, what information is missing or what could be tested? Leave with an action, not just a general opinion. Assess your organisation by reports sent on the agreed date and files with no next step. These indicators track your work; they predict neither sales nor income.

Frequently asked questions about seller updates

Should you send an update when there are no viewings?

Yes, in this suggested approach: explain completed actions, missing information and the next step, without inventing buyer interest.

Is a spreadsheet enough to start?

Yes, if you keep it updated and can find deadlines easily. The best starting point is the tool you actually use.

When should you discuss a price adjustment?

When you have evidence to discuss with the seller: consistent feedback, relevant property comparisons and a review of the presentation. The absence of a viewing alone is not enough to diagnose the problem.
